About the workshop

How do data, computing, and communication infrastructure shape what an autonomous physical system can observe, decide, and do?

A vehicle approaching an intersection, a robot on a factory floor, and an autonomous vessel all have a limited window to act. The dynamics of each task determine how fresh its observations must be, how much computation it can afford, and how quickly information must travel.

D-CAPS brings together researchers in data systems, computer architecture, and networking to examine these dependencies. Alongside the Fifth IEEE International Conference on Mobility: Operations, Services, and Technologies, the workshop explores which design principles carry across land, air, and maritime systems.

Call for papers

We invite original work from academia, industry, and government on infrastructure within a single platform or across multiple systems. Foundational research, early results, prototypes, measurement studies, and deployment experience are welcome, including general-purpose architectures and services with applications to autonomous physical systems.

Topics of interest

Data infrastructure

Systems for acquiring, storing, managing, and processing data.

Computing infrastructure

Hardware and software architectures for onboard, edge, and cloud computing.

Communication infrastructure

Architectures and protocols for exchanging information within and across systems.

Sensing and actuation infrastructure

Hardware and software that allow autonomous physical systems to observe and act on their environment.

AI and machine learning infrastructure

Systems for training, deploying, and serving models.

Systems architecture and integration

Design and integration of sensing, computing, communication, and control.

Infrastructure management and orchestration

Methods for provisioning, configuring, and managing data, computing, and communication resources for autonomous physical systems.

Interoperability and standards

Interfaces and standards that allow components from different vendors or platforms to work together.

Dependability, security, and privacy

Methods for maintaining reliable operation and protecting systems and data.

Testbeds, measurement, and deployment

Experimental platforms, evaluation methods, and lessons from real-world deployments.

Submission guidelines

Submit a PDF of up to six pages in double-column IEEE conference format. The limit includes figures, tables, references, acknowledgments, and appendices. Number the pages, embed fonts, and check that illustrations are legible in grayscale.

Review is single-blind. Include author names and affiliations. Submit original, unpublished work that is not under review at another archival venue.

With all authors’ approval, enter the title, abstract, complete author list, affiliations, and conflicts of interest in HotCRP. Contact the co-chairs for post-deadline changes.

Review and publication

Each paper will receive at least three independent reviews. The committee will evaluate originality, relevance, technical soundness, clarity, and the evidence supporting the claims. Evidence may include analysis, simulation, hardware experiments, or field studies.

At least one author of each accepted paper must register and present in person. Authors must submit the camera-ready PDF and complete the IEEE publication forms.

Accepted and presented papers will appear in the IEEE MOST 2027 proceedings and will be submitted to IEEE Xplore.
